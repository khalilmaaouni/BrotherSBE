Why another Claude Code plugin

Most of what exists in this space is good, and most of it is solving a different problem. The question is not which is better. It is which layer your team is missing.

Three of those rows are hard to copy, because they are structural rather than a matter of writing better prompts.

Absent evidence is a verdict. Enforced when a check is written, not when it runs: a check declaring PASS as its empty state is refused registration, and a meta-test walks the registries rather than a written list.

Every rule names its enforcer. Each law ends in [checked: some_tool.py] or [human]. That is not a documentation convention, it changes what you are permitted to believe. The spine itself is [human].

The ceremony is computed. Five objective questions, first match wins, and the declared tier is re-derived from the diff and compared. A tool that lets the person in a hurry decide how much rigour applies is a tool that applies none on the day it matters.

What it does not do

Stated here rather than discovered in month two.

  • It does not deploy. No apply rights on production state, ever. It drafts the exact command and its rollback for a human to run.
  • It does not notice that a change needed an approval. The gate verifies an approval that was declared. A money-path change that declares nothing sails past it. What you need there is CODEOWNERS and branch protection, and neither ships here.
  • It does not prove two derivations are independent. It proves their text differs. That is a floor.
  • It does not resolve a rehearsal identifier against your job system. A human follows that pointer.
  • It does not judge whether a stated reason is a good one. It checks that a reviewable reason exists and is not a refused token.
  • It does not measure the spine. "Design before verification" and "install the check before writing the work" are disciplines. No tool computes whether you followed them.
  • It has no opinion on visual design, typography or accessibility. Different toolchain, no overlap.
  • It does not replace your judgement about your own domain. The datamodel check enforces that every entity names an owner. It cannot tell you that you named the wrong one.

A control whose limits are hidden is a control you will over-trust at exactly the wrong moment.
