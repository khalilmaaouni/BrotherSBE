## The four that matter most in practice

**Nothing detects that a change needed an approval.** The approval gate
verifies an approval that was *declared*. A money-path change that declares
nothing sails past it. If that matters to you, the control you need is a
CODEOWNERS file and branch protection on your own repository, and neither
ships here.

**Two derivations differing in text is a floor, not a proof.** The numbers
gate checks that the second derivation is not a copy-paste of the first. It
does not read which tables each one touches. Two queries against the same
broken view will agree with each other perfectly.

**A rehearsal id is a pointer, not a resolution.** Nothing here calls your job
system to confirm that the rehearsal actually ran. A human follows the id.

**This repository's own merged pull requests carry no independent review.**
Applied to its own history, the approval law reports NO-DATA, not PASS. That is
disclosed rather than hidden, and it is the honest answer to "does the project
practise what it sells" on that one axis: not yet, on this axis, and it says so.

## What is a control and what is a discipline

The distinction runs through everything, and it is the single most useful lens
for reading this project.

- **[checked: tool]** means a script decides it and CI can block on it.
- **[human]** means nothing computes it. The line is a stated discipline.

The spine itself is `[human]`. "Design before verification" and "install the
check before writing the work" are not enforced by anything. Reading a rule
without knowing which class it is in recreates exactly the overclaim these
laws were rewritten to remove.

## The measurement caveat

Every threshold this project ships, and every baseline in its rubric, was
measured on one estate. One. They are starting points, not constants, and the
honest instruction is to re-measure them on yours.

## What that honesty is for

It would be easy to write a page that said all of this was solved. The reason
not to is practical rather than moral: a team that discovers an
overclaimed control at the moment it fails does not adjust its confidence in
that one control. It switches the whole thing off.

Stating the limits is what makes the rest of the claims worth anything.
